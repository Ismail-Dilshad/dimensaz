# Short-form editing

Choose one audience, one promise and one payoff. In the first 1–3 seconds,
show the result, present a visible problem, or open with a specific question.
Deliver the payoff before the call to action. Give each cut, camera move or
motion insert a job in the story.

Pacing: divide the length by the narration beats; give every beat a visual action
that demonstrates it. A new visual event lands at least every 1–2 seconds, scenes
run 2–5 seconds, and nothing on screen is still for more than 1.5 seconds. For
30 seconds plan about 70–80 spoken words and 6–8 scenes. Join scenes with designed
transitions (wipe, morph, zoom, mask), never a fade through an empty frame. Before
delivery run `pnpm tempoloom media pacing FILE`.

For footage, log source timecodes and select takes. Establish the dialogue cut
and remove false starts or dead time while preserving intelligibility. Use
picture and sound cuts independently when a J-cut or L-cut helps continuity.
Check framing, exposure and color across cuts. Place captions inside the safe
area and time them to speech evidence. Mix dialogue first and audition the full
export for distracting transitions, masking and audible edit seams.

For new motion, choose a physical action that communicates the beat. Design
the material, camera, lighting, contact, reaction and settling hold. Generate or
source isolated realistic 3D objects and moving parts. A reusable neutral master
can support a different arrangement or mechanism. Flat labels and captions
serve the viewer when needed. For an existing design revision, honor the
requested continuity and preserve the original files.

Check platform-specific safe areas and export constraints against current
official TikTok, Instagram or YouTube guidance when they matter. For trends,
record the date and source and use the observations to inform the hook and
pacing. Study a reference's timing or staging and apply the mechanism to the
new subject with original art and choreography.
