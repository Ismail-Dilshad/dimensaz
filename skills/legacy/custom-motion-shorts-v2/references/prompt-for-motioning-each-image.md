# Motion brief for each section

Score the whole layered scene: establish the set, move the actor, let its companion respond, then hold. Select the single change the viewer needs to see at each beat. Write down the before and after states. If the meaning is clear in a still, hold it. Select one duration within the card's range and use the card stage’s smoothstep easing throughout the video.

> At [scene-relative cue], [named part] moves from [start position/state] to [end position/state] over [0.4–0.6 s] to explain [relationship]. Its fixed attachment is [pivot/anchor]. Hold [stationary objects, type and camera] steady. After it settles, [optional smaller support] changes once at [later cue] because [specific meaning]. Reveal [exact complete phrase] at [evidence-bound voice cue or silent display cue]. Keep it legible through [hold endpoint]. End with [named spatial anchor] aligned for the next shot.

**Worked example.** On “the price fits”, let the beam rotate five degrees to level in 0.5 s. Hold its stand still. After the beam settles, bring the small price marker into view over 0.45 s. Leave the beam level as the phrase finishes. Place the next hero on the same centerline. If only a flattened image exists, request the separate beam before specifying its rotation.

Record each motion's start, end, relative importance and reason. Stagger supports. Allow one dominant change at a time. Keep the background and text stationary through a complex object action. Animate a phrase as one shaped unit. Keep independent layers free of trails, letter splitting and camera shake. A match cut needs two compatible silhouettes or a shared anchor in the same screen position. Inspect immediately before and after the cut.

Add one soft, cleared sound only when the action benefits from it. Name its material, cue and gain. Retain voice priority. Mark unreviewed listening explicitly.
