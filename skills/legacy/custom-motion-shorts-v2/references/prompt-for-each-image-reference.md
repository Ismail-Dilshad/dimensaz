# Image brief for each section

Start with the **exact phrase** the audience needs to understand. Explain the cause or decision in one sentence. Search the selected card's poster and motion categories for an example of its light, material or perspective. Record the filename and hash. Use the library's original briefs as historical evidence.

Give each section a unique object ID and a plain subject name. Check the ledger for both names and visual synonyms. A phone shown from another angle still counts as the same phone. For a deliberate callback, identify the previous beat and what the viewer learns from seeing it again.

Before writing prompts, list the anchor filename, focal object, receiver or destination, signal, depth order, and contact point. Write a separate isolated-asset prompt for each needed layer, using identical lighting and viewpoint across the set:

> Create [object in its exact starting state] for [the script's idea]. Use the [card] material, [named surface colors and hexes], [lighting direction and softness], and [viewpoint] from [reference filename]. Its visible distinguishing detail is [one detail that conveys the idea]. Show the complete silhouette, including [edge/handle/attachment]. Leave transparent padding on every side. A separate [moving part, if needed] joins at [attachment]. Deliver a text-free cutout. The compositor supplies the background, contact shadow and Sorani phrase.

For example, a price comparison in **Blue Editorial** could use a white-enamel balance on a cool studio surface. Request the beam as a separate blue-tipped part and give its midpoint as the pivot. A following CTA could use an envelope with a visible message edge. Keep the enamel and light direction consistent. Choose the second object for the new action.

In the storyboard record the intended hero zone, support zone, exact phrase, highlighted words and final scale. Keep the upper/lower or left/right zone assignment consistent across the requested format. Inspect transparent edges against the final background. Reject blank screens, unidentifiable props, clipped extremities and render-style substitutions.
