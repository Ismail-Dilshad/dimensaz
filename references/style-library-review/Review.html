Style cards

Classic

Feel. A clear, friendly narrated explanation with space between the spoken thought and the object illustrating it.

Picture. White #FFFFFF, cool mist #F3F6FC, pale grid #D5E0F7, cobalt #1F5FFF and navy #0B1B3A. Keep the square grid, pale blue corner wash and small glossy 3D objects with cool contact shadows. Dark RTL copy and white type on a blue chip carry the emphasis. Keep the chip attached to the meaningful phrase.

Movement. In the actual export, the cart and shop trade prominence, words accumulate with the voice, the megaphone sends rings, then a paper plane leaves the phone. For the improved Classic, use a 0.45–0.55 s slide to a shared anchor, reveal the intact phrase on its first spoken cue, and hold. Give each section a new symbol. Show visible content whenever a device enters.

Use for. A shop owner explaining a service, a narrated FAQ, or a local business seeking a calm, familiar introduction. Explicit “Classic” always selects this card. The existing working render is immediately available through the card's original project. The improved demonstration belongs to Phase 3.

Source. Original Classic video · Motion study

Blue Editorial

Feel. A bright tabletop demonstration in which one physical mechanism makes an abstract idea visible.

Picture. Cool white #F3F6FC, white #FFFFFF, cobalt #1F5FFF, navy #0B1B3A and pale blue #BBD0FF. Pale grid and diagonal guides sit behind a large white resin, glass or blue-enamel hero. Use a bold navy headline with one squared blue phrase block. Preserve clear separation where the reference lets the hero cover letters.

Movement. The style-2 directions describe a dart seating and a slider reaching a stop. The filmed blue badge swings on a lanyard, the balance tilts, and blue disks or panels cover the frame on exit. Some clips switch text mid-shot. For a tutorial, give the movable part 0.45–0.55 s to reach its endpoint, hold the housing and type still, and match the next object to the same anchor.

Use for. First-time learners, product walkthroughs and explanations of how something works. Alias style-1 selects discovery subjects. Alias style-2 selects mechanisms. Alias styles-with-video-1 selects the filmed staging examples. They share a card because their materials, grid, blue blocks and studio light match.

Sources. Style-1 glass question bulb and magnet, style-2 control panel and jigsaw, and styles-with-video-1 hanging badge and scale. Discovery posters · Mechanism posters · Blue filmstrips

Red Signal

Feel. A decisive visual diagnosis. A bright red component makes the problem or direction immediately legible.

Picture. Black #0A0A0B, charcoal #17171A, grid #1E1E22, red #E5202E and white #FFFFFF. Use a near-black grid, a narrow pool of red floor light and reflective black metal or transparent glass. Keep most lettering white. Set one short phrase in a red rectangular tab.

Movement. Style-3’s written compass direction calls for a needle to slow onto its bearing. Style-6 supplies stills of an odometer and enlarged iris without motion instructions. Proposed motion for either variant is one 0.4–0.5 s rotation, opening or register change. Let the dial housing and headline hold, then match the circular geometry into the next beat.

Use for. A diagnostic question, a warning, or a surprising business metric directed at an analytical audience. Style-3 emphasizes direction and access. Style-6 emphasizes measurement and faults. The same red light, grid, hardware finish and tabs justify their shared card.

Sources. Style-3 compass, vault and key. Style-6 odometer, bar chart and magnifier. Direction posters · Diagnosis posters

Apricot Miniatures

Feel. A warm, human-scale first step. Small figures and tactile controls make action feel approachable.

Picture. Cream #FAF5EC, peach #FFD3B3, orange #FF6A1A, espresso #1A1411 and sand #E2D2B8. A lightly dotted cream ground supports sculpted ivory steps, a tiny shop or a rounded orange button. Use dark, rounded RTL headlines and espresso lettering on orange emphasis blocks for readability.

Movement. Only posters document this family. Proposed action: hold the button base still while the fingertip depresses the cap over 0.45–0.55 s. Cut on its settled circular shape to the pin in the next section. Keep the dots and headline fixed.

Use for. Welcoming first-time customers, explaining a practical next step, or encouraging someone who feels stuck. Alias style-4.

Sources. Staircase, hand and button, shopkeeper and hovering map pin. Poster evidence

Emerald Contrast

Feel. A measured comparison in which two objects make a distinction easier to understand.

Picture. Forest #06201A, secondary green #0B5C45, dark grid #0F3A30, lime #C6F432 and pearl #F4FFF0. Use a deep green studio floor, pearl ceramic or pale metal, and one lime detail. In the posters the sphere/pyramid and eye/bag scale carry the comparison. Use pearl lettering, with forest type on a lime phrase band.

Movement. No clips or written motion accompany the numbered folder. Proposed action: settle one pan of the scale over 0.5–0.6 s while the fulcrum, backdrop and copy stay still. Hold both sides long enough to compare them. Use the fulcrum as the handoff point.

Use for. Evaluating alternatives, explaining value, or helping a careful buyer weigh a decision. Alias style-5.

Sources. Balance scale, sphere/pyramid comparison, mannequin with measuring tape. Poster evidence

Lavender Glass

Feel. Light, quiet and reassuring. Refraction carries the visual interest without adding clutter.

Picture. Lavender white #F7F6FF, lilac #E9E4FF, blush #FFE8EE, violet #6D5BFF and ink #1C1B3A. A faint dot field sits under diffused lilac and peach light. Pair a pearl base with one transparent or frosted object. Use generous dark RTL type and a rounded violet highlight with a contrast-checked foreground.

Movement. The pictured gauge, hand-held gem and coin are still references. Proposed motion is a single 0.5–0.6 s level change inside a stationary glass cylinder. Hold the final reading, then align the next circular hero to the gauge rim.

Use for. Service onboarding, a gentle progress update, or a trust-building explanation for a new customer. Alias style-7.

Sources. Glass cylinder gauge, hand cupping a gem and coin entering a box. Poster evidence

Green Beacon

Feel. A luminous signal makes an otherwise hidden route or state visible.

Picture. Working swatches are black-green #0C100F, charcoal #262B29, emerald #18CB67, mint #86F6AF and ivory #F4F7F0. Use square grid, a local green spotlight and black props with an emissive edge. Pair white headlines with dark lettering on an emerald block. This family uses a greener light and darker objects than Emerald Contrast.

Movement. Film shows the badge turn, the question mark light up, the map pin recede along a path and the megaphone rings grow into an exit. The dark stage often holds while one local light changes. For a calm version, let one ring expand for 0.45–0.55 s and resolve into the next circular anchor.

Use for. Discovery, navigation, a system becoming active, or a tech-savvy audience following a signal. Aliases styles-with-video-2 and styles-with-video-6 resolve to the same card. The two directories are exact duplicates.

Sources. Name badge, map pin, shopfront/lock and green megaphone clips. Stills · Filmstrips

Tangerine Counter

Feel. A friendly storefront offer with a strong graphic silhouette and warm paper beneath it.

Picture. Working swatches are cream #FBF2DF, tangerine #FF651E, indigo #251D56, muted lavender #BDB5CB and paper shadow #D7C5A9. Use a faint cream grid, an occasional folded edge, a chunky molded sale tag or striped storefront and a grounded shadow. Pair bold indigo RTL type with an orange highlight slab.

Movement. The sale tag pivots edge-on and back, the shop front rises, the phone straightens and sound rings change size. Several samples hold the same composition for most of the clip. Use a single 0.45–0.55 s pivot that stops face-on, keep the headline still, then match the face of the tag to a card.

Use for. A local offer, a retail event or a short explainer for price-conscious shoppers. Alias styles-with-video-3.

Sources. Sale tag, shopfront, smartphone tilt and tangerine rings clips. Stills · Filmstrips

Selection and remix

Read the script for its actual topic, industry, audience, emotional turn and tone. Choose the card whose *visual action* explains the decisive sentence. Use the supplied name first when there is one. A calm practical introduction can use Apricot Miniatures and a warning about a metric can use Red Signal, even if both scripts concern the same shop.

The picker accepts the agent's five-field brief and a source script. Its offline scoring gives documented cues a reproducible weight and reports recent repeated visual families. The archive's other suggested palettes can inspire a remix. Record both parents, one borrowed element from each, the shared palette, and the rationale. Use one coherent setting throughout. Label the result as a new design. A close history match warrants editorial review, and an explicit name retains priority.

All source filenames, including those abbreviated in card descriptions, appear in the full inventory and indexed visual sheets. Production integration of the improved cards, prompt rewrite, packaging and three new renders follow the approval checkpoint.
